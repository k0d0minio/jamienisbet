# Objection Handling — Honest Answers, Not Hard Sells

> **ICM role:** Layer 4 — working (sales-kit). The pushbacks a seller hits and a plain answer in
> Jamie's voice. The throughline: **be honest, never push.** A referral kept warm beats one forced.

## "It's too expensive."
A one-page site starts at €200, once, and works for years. Set that against the customers a business
loses with no site — or a broken one. If €200 is still too much, it's probably not the right time, and
that's fine — leave them the link for later.

## "I'll just use Wix / Squarespace myself."
They can, and for some people that's the right call. The difference is the weekends spent fighting a
template that still looks like a template. Jamie does it properly, once, and they get their time back.
If they genuinely enjoy the DIY, don't push.

## "I already know someone who does websites."
No hard sell. Just mention Jamie's a senior engineer who handles the whole thing and quotes a clear
fixed price — handy if their guy goes quiet or it gets more technical. Leave the link; people remember
it when the first option falls through.

## "Not right now / maybe later."
Fine — don't chase. Ask what would make it the right time (a launch, a busy season, a rebrand) and check
back then. A soft "want me to send the link for whenever?" keeps the door open without pressure.

## "What if it goes wrong? I'm not technical."
That's the point — they never touch the technical side. Jamie scopes it, builds it, and supports it;
they deal with one person, not an agency. And the seller isn't on the hook for any of it either.

## "Can you do [something bigger than a landing page]?"
Yes — multi-page sites, booking systems, web apps, AI features, automations. That's where the seller
stops quoting and introduces Jamie: "Let me put you in touch — he'll scope it and give you a clear
price." The seller still earns their 10%.
