# Negotiation Playbook

> **ICM role:** Layer 3 — reference (proposals). The coach's tactics for Stage 03.
> Goal: close at a higher-than-usual rate without losing the deal. Honest, value-first, never tricky.

## 1. Anchor
- **Open high, with a reason.** Anchor at the value-based number from
  [`pricing-models.md`](pricing-models.md), justified by the client's upside — not "my rate is X".
  The hourly rate anchors at the €120 standard; there is no hourly premium — you open high on *value*.
- The first number framed sets the range; let the anchor be the *value*, then the price.
- Never anchor at the floor. The floor is private (in setup) — it's the walk line, not an opener.

## 2. Frame value over cost
- Translate price into the client's terms: "€X to unlock €Y" beats "€X for Z hours".
- Use their own success metric (from discovery) as the denominator.
- Cost of delay: what each week without it costs them.

## 3. Tier (good / better / best)
- Always present three options (see pricing-models). The **middle is the target**; the top makes the
  middle feel reasonable; the bottom protects the floor and keeps a "yes" available.
- Tiers differ by **scope and outcome**, not just hours.

## 4. Handle objections (acknowledge → reframe → option)
- **"Too expensive."** → "Compared to what?" Reframe to upside; offer the lower tier (less scope), not a discount.
- **"Can you do it cheaper?"** → trade price for something: longer timeline, bigger deposit, a retainer, a case study, a referral, or outcome upside (commission).
- **"We need to think."** → surface the real blocker; agree a decision date and the next step.
- **"Another quote is lower."** → compare scope and risk, not price; don't race to the bottom.

## 5. Hold or walk
- Hold firm above the floor. If the deal can't clear the floor on acceptable terms, **say walk** —
  a bad-rate client costs more than the gap.
- Walk-away is leverage *because* it's real: state it calmly, not as a threat.

## 6. Talk-track (prep exact words for)
- The opening / value frame.
- The anchor — the number **and the because**.
- The two objections you most expect.
- The close: "if I can do X, can we start Y?"
