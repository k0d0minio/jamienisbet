## 2. The decisions I should not make alone

### Q1 — CIRS code and coefficient: is **0.35** defensible for my work? *(the big one)*

My understanding: **0.75** applies to activities on the **Art. 151.º CIRS** table (engineer /
consultant-type professions); **0.35** applies to *"other services"* not on that list — and the AT
judges by the **substance** of the work, not the code typed at registration. On €50k gross that is a
taxable base of **€37,500 vs €17,500** — roughly halving IRS. It moves more money than any other
decision here.

My concern is that I present as a *"consultant/engineer"*, which points to **0.75**. The 0.35 case
looks strongest for genuine **software development output** and weakest for advisory consultancy —
and my work is genuinely a **mix**.

1. For my actual deliverables (build work vs. advisory), can you **defend 0.35** — and would you
   **put that position in writing**?
2. Does it make sense to **split by substance** — 0.35 for development, 0.75 for advisory, under
   matched codes — rather than one blanket code?
3. What is the **reclassification risk** (interest + penalties) if the AT reassesses to 0.75, and
   what evidence of deliverables would defend a 0.35 position?
4. Which **CAE and CIRS codes** do you actually recommend I register?

> **My stance:** if you will not back 0.35 for my real mix, I register clean at **0.75**. A shaky
> aggressive position is not worth the exposure — especially as the year-1/2 startup reduction
> compresses the 0.35 advantage early anyway (see Q2).

### Q2 — Start date: has waiting already cost me the −50% year? *(this one has got worse)*

My understanding: the **−50% year 1 / −25% year 2** coefficient reduction (Art. 31.º n.º 10 CIRS) is
applied per **calendar tax year**, not on a rolling 12 months. The **12-month Segurança Social
exemption**, by contrast, **is** rolling from registration and so is *not* affected by start date.

I had originally planned to file on **1 July 2026**. It is now **mid-August**. If I file now, my
−50% year (2026) covers only ~4 months of invoicing; if I wait until **1 January 2027**, the −50%
lands on a full year:

| | Start ~1 Sep 2026 | Start 1 Jan 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| −50% year | 2026 — base ≈ €6.3k on ~4 months' income | 2027 — base ≈ €18.8k on a full year |
| −25% year | 2027 — base ≈ €28.1k | 2028 — base ≈ €28.1k |
| First full-coefficient year | 2028 — base €37.5k | 2029 — base €37.5k |
| Taxable base across 2027–28 | ≈ **€65.6k** | ≈ **€46.9k** |

*(0.75 coefficient assumed, €50k gross, pre-credits.)* That difference is roughly **€6,000+ of IRS**
— but deferring means I cannot legally invoice between now and January, which I cannot afford.

1. Is my reading of "calendar tax year" for the reduction, and "rolling" for the SS exemption, **correct**?
2. Because the simplified regime taxes Cat. B on a **receipts** basis, does the **timing of receipt**
   give me any legitimate room — e.g. registering now but arranging that a larger share of income is
   *received* in 2027 — or is that a false economy / an abuse risk?
3. Is there any smarter filing date in the next few weeks (a month boundary, a quarter boundary)?
4. **Or is the honest answer simply: start immediately, accept the partial −50% window?** That is my
   expectation; I would like it confirmed rather than assumed.
